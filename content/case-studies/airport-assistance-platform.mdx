## Overview

empower is a platform for UK airports that helps passengers with reduced mobility get the assistance they need, whether that is a wheelchair, someone to guide them, or other support through the airport.

It brings everyone involved into one system. Passengers book assistance and follow their journey. Service providers receive and assign the requests. Ambassadors, the staff who help passengers in the terminal, see their tasks in a mobile app. Airport authorities track how well the service is delivered.

The platform is in pilot. I was the Technical Lead.

## The problem

Assistance for passengers with reduced mobility depends on several organisations working together: the passenger, the company providing the assistance, its staff on the ground and the airport. Bookings, staff and communication were spread across manual processes and separate channels.

For a passenger in a wheelchair, that can mean not knowing who is coming to help or when. For the airport, it means little visibility of waiting times or whether service targets are met.

## Who it serves

| User                    | What they do on the platform                                                               |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Passengers              | Book assistance, follow their journey, message and call their ambassador, rate the service |
| Service providers       | Receive and assign requests, manage ambassadors and equipment, watch service levels        |
| Ambassadors             | See assigned requests, update each step of the journey, stay in touch with the passenger   |
| Airport authorities     | Track performance and KPIs, manage terminals, pick-up areas and equipment                  |
| Platform administrators | Set up airports and service providers, manage users, roles and website content             |

## Six modules

1. **Public website**, with a content management system for the blog, FAQs and testimonials, and the privacy, GDPR and cookie policies.
2. **Passenger app**, on the web, Android and iOS.
3. **Service provider portal**, on the web.
4. **Ambassador app**, on Android and iOS.
5. **Airport portal**, on the web, for analytics and airport information.
6. **Internal management portal**, on the web, for platform administrators.

## The passenger journey

A passenger books assistance by choosing an assistance type, using the standard aviation codes for wheelchair and other help, then saying whether they need an ambassador or an assistive device, and selecting their flight. A journey with a connection can be booked as several legs at once.

Each request then moves through milestones that match what actually happens at the airport. These differ by journey:

- **Departure:** from confirmation and arrival at the airport, through check-in, baggage drop, security and the boarding gate, to boarding.
- **Arrival:** from the flight landing, through disembarking, baggage claim and customs, to leaving the airport.
- **Transit:** from arriving at the airport, through security and the transit gate, to the connecting flight.

Passengers, ambassadors and service providers update the same request as it moves along. Some details lock once a step has passed: the flight can't be changed after booking, for example, and the arrival time can't be edited after pickup.

## Staying in touch on the day

The platform's main purpose is to let passengers who need help reach the people helping them.

- **Messaging** between the passenger, the ambassador and the service provider, which closes a set time after the service is complete.
- **Live location**, shared both ways between passenger and ambassador on Google Maps, and switched off automatically when the service ends.
- **A call button** that alerts the assigned ambassador straight away.
- **A shareable journey link**, so family can follow where the passenger and ambassador are and each step they reach.
- **Notifications** for booking confirmations, reminders and flight or gate changes.

## Running the service

Service providers get the tools to deliver assistance on the day:

- Requests listed and filtered by status and urgency, with flight schedules to see the demand coming.
- Ambassadors and equipment, such as wheelchairs, assigned to each request.
- Ambassador availability and breaks, with shifts brought in from the provider's own HR system.
- An inventory of wheelchairs and other equipment, including repairs.
- Requests created on a passenger's behalf when they arrive without booking or call a hotline.
- One login across several airports.

## Service levels and analytics

Every request is tracked against service-level targets. Departure, arrival and transit requests are listed separately and colour-coded green, amber or red by how long the passenger has been waiting, with different targets for pre-booked and unbooked passengers.

Airports get KPI dashboards for departure wait time, arrival wait time and transit time, alongside service ratings, complaint resolution and equipment availability.

## Platform architecture

empower is built as microservices in Laravel and Spring. React powers the web portals and Flutter the passenger and ambassador apps. Data is stored in MySQL and a NoSQL database, real-time updates go through Ably, and maps and location use Google Maps. It runs on DigitalOcean.

## Engineering challenges

**One request, many hands.** A single assistance request is updated by the passenger, the service provider and the ambassador, across departure, arrival and transit journeys with many steps each. The rules for who can change what, and when, had to hold for every step.

**Real time, and only for as long as needed.** Messages, live location and call alerts have to arrive instantly while a passenger is being helped, and stop once the service is complete, so a passenger's location isn't shared longer than needed.

**Many organisations on one platform.** Several airports and service providers use the same system, and each should see only its own requests, staff and equipment, while service providers can still work across more than one airport.

**Measuring service honestly.** Service-level tracking depends on accurate timestamps for every step, on different targets for pre-booked and unbooked passengers, and on recording delays caused by the passenger separately from delays in the service.

**Accessible by design.** The people using the passenger app are the ones most likely to need accessibility support, so the app includes its own settings for font size, colours and magnification.

**Privacy.** The platform holds personal and travel details for people who may have disabilities. It needed GDPR consent and account deletion, OTP verification for sign-up and for changing a phone number or email, and an audit trail of user activity.

## My role

As Technical Lead, I led the engineering of the platform across its six modules, working from the business requirements through to the pilot.

## Where it stands

empower is in pilot at UK airports. Its purpose is simple: a passenger in a wheelchair, or anyone who needs help getting through the airport, can reach the staff assisting them, know who is coming and where they are, and get through the airport with confidence.
